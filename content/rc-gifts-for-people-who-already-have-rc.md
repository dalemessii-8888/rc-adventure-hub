Buying for someone who's already in the hobby is harder than buying for a beginner, because they know what they like and they probably own more gear than you can see. The mistake to avoid is buying something they already have, or something that doesn't fit the ecosystem they've built.

Two approaches work. Either **go sideways**: give them a vehicle in a category they don't own yet (a boat for the car person, a glider for the boat person). Or **go up**: give them a step up from what they're currently running. Both are covered below.

## First, find out what they own

Casually learn what they already run, and what they've been eyeing. RC gear tends to come with its own battery types and connectors, so accessories like batteries and chargers are easy to get wrong without that knowledge. If you can't find out, a complete vehicle in a *different* category is the lowest-risk way to avoid duplicating gear.

## What we'd skip

**Accessories you can't verify.** Batteries, chargers, radios and electronics depend on the exact system the person uses. Unless you know precisely what they run, a complete vehicle is a safer gift than a part.

**Bigger isn't automatically better.** A step up should match the skill they have now, not the skill you hope they'll develop.
