Every RC category -- cars, planes, and boats -- splits into the same two broad power systems: electric (battery-powered) and nitro (small glow-fuel engines, sometimes loosely called "gas"). Almost every beginner should start electric, but it's worth actually understanding why rather than just taking that on faith.

## The case for electric (the right default for almost everyone)

- **Lower hassle.** Charge a battery, plug it in, drive/fly/boat. No fuel mixing, no engine tuning, no glow igniter.
- **Cheaper to run.** Batteries are a fixed cost you recharge hundreds of times. Nitro fuel is a recurring cost every single session.
- **Quieter.** Genuinely important if you're running near houses, at a park, or anywhere noise complaints are a realistic concern. Electric RC vehicles are dramatically quieter than nitro.
- **Cleaner.** No exhaust residue coating your vehicle and your hands after every run.
- **Better beginner-assist tech.** The electronic stabilization systems mentioned in our RC plane guide, for instance, are an electric-only feature -- nitro planes don't have an equivalent.

## The case for nitro (a legitimate choice, just not usually a first choice)

- **Longer runtime per fill-up** than a comparable battery charge, for a lot of nitro vehicles.
- **A different, mechanically-involved hobby in itself.** Some hobbyists specifically want the engine-tuning, fuel-mixing, wrench-turning side of nitro as part of the appeal -- if that sounds fun rather than tedious, that's a real reason to consider it.
- **A distinct sound and feel** that some hobbyists genuinely prefer, particularly in cars and boats.

## Our actual recommendation

Start electric. It removes almost every point of friction that causes beginners to quit a new hobby in the first month -- noise complaints, fuel hassle, engine maintenance, higher running costs. If, after a season of electric RC, you find yourself specifically wanting the engine-tuning side of the hobby, nitro is a well-supported and completely reasonable second vehicle to add -- but as a second purchase made with more experience, not a first one made on faith.
