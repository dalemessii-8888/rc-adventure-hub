A young adult is the easiest person to buy RC gear for and the easiest to get wrong. They don't need a "kid" toy, and they'll notice the difference between something that feels like a hobby and something that feels like a gimmick. The question isn't "how fast is it?" It's "where will they actually use it, and will it keep working?"

This guide is for the aunt, parent, sibling or partner doing the buying. We sort the picks by how the person lives, because that's what decides which one will get used.

## First, find out if they already have one

If you can ask without spoiling the surprise, do. Someone who already owns an RC car doesn't need a first RC car. They might love a different category (a boat, say) or a step-up like the Losi Micro-B below. If you can't ask, stick to the "first RC" picks, which are safe because they're not duplicates of anything a beginner owns.

## Pick by where they live

### They have a yard, a park or open ground nearby: Traxxas Slash 4X4

This is the most-recommended first hobby-grade RC for a reason: it's tough, replacement parts are everywhere, and 4WD handles grass, gravel and dirt. Two things to know before you buy. The Amazon.ca listing is the higher-spec "Ultimate" trim, and it does **not** include a battery or charger, so factor those in and check they match the car.

{{product:traxxas-slash-4x4}}

### Same idea, a bit more forgiving: ARRMA Granite

A hobby-grade truck with proper parts support for less than the Slash. It's heavier and slower, which makes it more forgiving for a newer driver. Check what's included on the listing before you order, since we haven't confirmed it.

{{product:arrma-granite}}

### They already drive RC and want something different: Losi Micro-B 2WD Buggy

This is a 1/24 micro buggy, not a scaled-down truck. It's for cornering and speed, with an aluminum chassis and real oil-filled shocks, not rough backyard bashing. It's a poor first car for a total beginner and a good gift for someone who's already hooked and wants a cheap way into racing-style driving.

{{product:losi-micro-b}}

### They live near a lake or pond: BEZGAR TX123 or ARRIS Jet Boat

A boat is a great gift for someone near water, and a bad one for someone who isn't, so be honest about access.

The **BEZGAR TX123** is not a premium brushless boat, but it's well-reviewed, self-rights after a 180-degree capsize, and ships with two batteries, a carrying case and LED lights for evening runs.

{{product:bezgar-tx123}}

The **ARRIS RC Jet Boat** uses water-jet drive instead of an exposed propeller, so it handles weeds and shallow water that would foul a normal boat. It's also self-righting and ships with two batteries.

{{product:arris-jet-boat}}

More options and the self-righting checklist are in [Best RC Boats for Adults](/boats/best-rc-boats-for-adults/).

### They want a challenge and have open space: VOLANTEXRC Sport Cub 500mm

Planes have a steeper learning curve than cars or boats, and crashes are part of it. This one is a good first plane because its assist system has three levels you can dial back as skills improve, and its prop saver absorbs crash impact. It needs an open field and calm weather, so it's a gift for the right person, not everyone.

{{product:volantex-sport-cub}}

### They live in a small apartment: Hylukon 1:43 Desktop Drift Car

If outdoor space isn't an option, this is the best "try RC" gift. It's a palm-sized drift car with a gyro and interchangeable tires, built for a desk or coffee table. It's also a good add-on to a bigger present.

{{product:hylukon-drift-car}}

## Things that make the gift land better

- **Make sure it works on day one.** Confirm batteries and a charger are included or bought separately, and read the charging instructions yourself so you can help them get started.
- **Put the "what's in the box" list in the card** if there's something they need to buy later (like a Slash battery and charger). It's much better than a surprise on gift day.
- **Check the delivery date** on the listing before you buy, especially in December.
