Buying an RC car for a teenager is easy to get wrong in two opposite ways. Spend a lot on something fast and fragile and it's in pieces by New Year's. Buy the cheapest thing with a "70 MPH!" sticker and it's a disappointment by the weekend. This guide is for the adult doing the buying: what to look for, what's actually in the box, and which cars match which kind of teen.

## The one distinction that decides whether the gift lasts

**Toy-grade** RC cars are sealed units. When something breaks, the whole car goes in the bin. **Hobby-grade** RC cars are built from replaceable parts, so a snapped part is a small repair rather than the end of the gift. For a teen who'll drive it hard, hobby-grade is almost always worth the extra money, which is why most of the picks below are hobby-grade. We've included one toy-grade pick and one desk-sized pick on purpose, because "will they even like this?" is a fair question to ask before spending more.

(For the long version, see [Best RC Cars for Adults](/cars/best-rc-cars-for-adults/) and [RC Hobby 101](/guides/rc-hobby-101-beginners-guide/). Everything on this page is battery-powered, which is the easier path for a first RC; if you're curious about nitro (fuel-powered) models, [Nitro vs. Electric](/guides/nitro-vs-electric/) explains why we'd start electric.)

## Check these four things before you order

1. **Where will they drive it?** A backyard, park or empty lot is ideal. If the answer is "a bedroom," skip to the desk drift car below.
2. **What's in the box?** This is the number one gift-day disappointment. Some cars arrive without a battery or charger. Each pick below says what we know is included; if it isn't stated, confirm on the Amazon.ca listing.
3. **What's the recommended age on the listing?** Manufacturers set their own age guidance. Read it on the listing and match it to your teen.
4. **Who's going to charge it?** Teens can handle this, but read the charging instructions together the first time (see the battery safety note below).

## Our picks

### Best overall gift for a teen who's new to RC: Redcat Racing Volcano EPX

If you want one answer, this is it. It's a real hobby-grade truck, not a toy, and unlike a lot of budget RTRs it arrives with the battery, charger and radio included, so on gift day there's nothing else to buy and nothing to wait for. The trade-off is thinner parts availability and a less refined feel than Traxxas or ARRMA. That's fine for a teen who's finding out whether they love this, and less fine if you want a car that grows with them for years.

{{product:redcat-volcano-epx}}

### Best if they'll still be driving it next year: Traxxas Slash 4X4

The Slash is the most-recommended first hobby-grade RC for good reasons: it's tough, parts are sold almost everywhere, and 4WD handles grass, gravel and dirt far better than 2WD. Two things to know as the buyer. The Amazon.ca listing is the higher-spec "Ultimate" trim, and it does **not** include a battery or charger, so a teen who unwraps it can't drive it until you've bought those too. Check that your battery and charger match the car before you buy, and if you're unsure, ask in the Q&A on the listing or check the manual.

{{product:traxxas-slash-4x4}}

### A forgiving middle path: ARRMA Granite

The Granite is a hobby-grade truck with real parts support for less money than the Slash. It's heavier and slower, which makes it a little more forgiving for a newer driver. We haven't confirmed exactly what's included in the Amazon.ca listing, so check that before ordering.

{{product:arrma-granite}}

### Best low-risk "let's see if they like it" gift: DEERC 9309E

We're not going to pretend this is hobby-grade: it's a sealed-unit toy-grade truck, so when something breaks you replace the truck. But it's honest about being a budget backyard basher, it's genuinely waterproof, and it ships with two batteries. If you're not sure the interest will last, this is the cheaper way to find out, and a good pick for a younger teen.

### Best for a bedroom or a small apartment: Hylukon 1:43 Desktop Drift Car

This isn't a driveway car. It's a palm-sized desk drift car with a gyro, swappable tires and LED lights, built to slide around on a desk or coffee table. It's the easiest "try this hobby" gift for a teen with no outdoor space, and it works as a stocking stuffer next to a bigger gift.

## What to buy alongside it

- **A second battery.** Most RTR cars give you roughly one short session per charge, and a spare roughly doubles the fun per outing. Match it to the exact car: connector type and size matter, so check the manual or the manufacturer's parts list rather than guessing.
- **A proper charger** if one isn't included (the Slash listing doesn't include one).
- **A small box of spare parts** for the car you choose. Look up "[model name] replacement parts" before you buy so you know they exist.

We don't link specific batteries here because getting a compatible one wrong is worse than not buying one. Check your model's manual first.

## What we'd skip

Anything sold mostly on top speed ("70 MPH!!") without any mention of chassis material, replacement parts or what's in the box. High speed on a cheap plastic chassis is a car that breaks in the first week. If the listing doesn't mention replacement parts, treat it as toy-grade.
