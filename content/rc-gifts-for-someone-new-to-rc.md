Giving RC to a total beginner is a bet on whether the first session goes well. If they're driving, floating or flying within minutes and nothing breaks, you've started a hobby. If they spend an evening hunting for the right charger, you've bought a box that lives in a closet.

So for a first-timer, the rules are different from the rest of this site: **forgiving beats fast, complete beats impressive, and small beats big.** Every pick here is chosen to make that first session go smoothly.

## The one rule: it has to work on day one

Before anything else, confirm what's in the box. A first-timer won't know to ask whether batteries and a charger are included, and they shouldn't have to find out the hard way. Our [RC gift checklist](/gifts/rc-gift-checklist/) walks through it, but the short version is that the picks below either include what's needed or are flagged where they don't.

## What to avoid for a first-timer

**Nitro or gas models.** They need fuel, tuning and patience. Electric is the right call for essentially every beginner gift; see [Nitro vs. Electric](/guides/nitro-vs-electric/).

**Big, fast brushless monsters.** They're exciting to watch and easy to crash. Start smaller; they can upgrade later.

**Complete unknowns.** If a listing is vague about what's included, pick something else.

## If you want to give a little extra

A short note in the card helps more than you'd think: tell them what's in the box, where it's best used, and that it's fine to start slow. And if you're giving a boat or plane in winter, say plainly that it's one to try in spring.
