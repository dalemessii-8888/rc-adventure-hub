Most disappointing RC gifts fail for boring reasons, not because the vehicle was bad. This checklist covers the things worth confirming before you buy, so the person you're gifting to can have fun on the day instead of troubleshooting.

## 1. Are the battery and charger in the box?

This is the biggest one. RC listings are inconsistent: some include everything, some include a battery but no charger, and some include neither. The listing is the only reliable source, so read the "what's in the box" section rather than assuming.

Real examples from this site's picks: the Traxxas Slash 4X4 listing on Amazon.ca is the higher-spec "Ultimate" trim and does **not** include a battery or charger.

{{product:traxxas-slash-4x4}}

By contrast, the Redcat Volcano EPX ships with the battery, charger and radio included.

{{product:redcat-volcano-epx}}

If the listing doesn't make it clear, assume it's not included and either choose a different vehicle or plan to buy the missing parts. If you do buy them separately, make sure they match the vehicle, since RC batteries and connectors aren't universal.

## 2. Hobby-grade or toy-grade?

Hobby-grade vehicles have replaceable parts and a support ecosystem, so they can be repaired and upgraded. Toy-grade ones are cheaper and simpler but generally aren't built to be repaired. Neither is wrong, but it's worth knowing which you're buying, and matching it to the person. A casual "drive it around" gift is fine as toy-grade; a gift for a future hobbyist should be hobby-grade.

The DEERC 9309E is an honest example of the toy-grade end: waterproof and with two included batteries, but not built around a long-term parts ecosystem.

{{product:deerc-9309e}}

For more on what the difference means in practice, see [RC Hobby 101](/guides/rc-hobby-101-beginners-guide/).

## 3. Does the skill level match the person?

Be honest about where the recipient is. A forgiving first vehicle (self-righting boat, gyro-assisted plane, small desktop car) turns a first session into a good memory. A fast, big or fragile one turns it into a repair job. If in doubt, go smaller.

## 4. Where will they actually use it?

- **Trucks and cars:** need a yard, driveway or park.
- **Boats:** need open water where RC boats are allowed. Check local rules, since some parks and lakes restrict them.
- **Planes:** need a big, open, calm area, and the less wind the better.
- **Desktop drift cars:** need only a table.

If the recipient has nowhere sensible to use it, pick something else.

## 5. Is it a gift for now, or for spring?

If it's cold where they live, boats and planes may be a gift for spring. Batteries generally perform worse in the cold, and a boat needs unfrozen water. That's fine, but say so in the card so they know it isn't broken or a miss. If you want something they can use right away, favour trucks and desktop cars.

## 6. Delivery and returns

Prices, stock and delivery dates change quickly in the holiday season. Check the Amazon.ca listing for current availability and delivery estimates before you order, and look at the return window in case it doesn't suit. Don't leave it to the last week.

## 7. A little extra goes a long way

A short note in the card helps: what's in the box, where it's best used, and that it's fine to start slow. If the vehicle needs batteries or a charger that you're supplying separately, tell them.
